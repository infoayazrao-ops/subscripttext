What is a subscript and superscript in chemistry?

In chemistry, a subscript is the small number that tells you how many atoms of an element are in a molecule: H₂O has two hydrogen atoms; CO₂ has two oxygen atoms. A superscript is used for ion charges and mass numbers (SO₄²⁻), not for atom counts. If you raise the 2 in H2O you get H²O, which is the wrong notation.

For classroom examples of both styles, see subscript vs superscript. To paste formulas, use the subscript generator in Formula mode.
