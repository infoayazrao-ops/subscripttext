Looking for a lowered b?

Quick answer: Unicode has no standard Latin subscript b. Tap ᵦ (Greek beta) or B₂ if you meant boron with a 2.

Published 24 August 2026

Closest to a lowered b

f is the same story: no Latin lowered f. Use f₂ or leave f normal.

Why Word still “works” and chat does not

Word can shrink any letter with Format → Subscript. That style dies in WhatsApp. Only characters that exist in Unicode travel. The official Latin set (U+2090–U+209C) is short: ₐ ₑ ₒ ₓ ₕ ₖ ₗ ₘ ₙ ₚ ₛ ₜ plus schwa. Extra phonetic marks (ᵢ ⱼ ᵣ ᵤ ᵥ) exist in some fonts and fail in others.

Math people often want x_b or a_f. Those are written as xᵦ or x with a normal b if the reader’s font lacks beta. In a paper you can keep Word styling. In a chat or a slide that will be copied out, pick a letter that exists (ₓ ₙ) or keep the index as a normal letter.

Our converter maps only the official Latin set above. Type “b” or “f” and they stay b and f. That is deliberate. Inventing a fake glyph would show as a box on half the phones in a class WhatsApp group.

On a phone, tap ᵦ or B₂, switch to the chat or slide, long-press, Paste. On a computer, click the chip, then Ctrl + V or Cmd + V. If you see a box, the font lacks that mark — keep a normal b rather than forcing a fake subscript.

Use B₂ when the 2 is a real index (boron, a second item). Use ᵦ when you only need the look of a small b and the reader will accept Greek. For homework that must stay Latin, leave b normal and say so once in the caption.
