What is the difference between subscript and superscript?

What Is a Subscript?

A subscript shows something attached to the bottom of a symbol. Examples:

  • Chemistry: H₂O, CO₂ (the 2 is subscript)
  • Math: x₁, x₂ (indices)
  • Physics: v₀ (initial values)

People often ask what is a subscript when writing lab reports or homework. Unicode subscript lets you copy and paste these symbols without special software. More digits: subscript numbers & symbols.

What Is Superscript?

Superscript raises text above the line. Examples:

  • Exponents: x², 10³
  • Footnote markers: word¹
  • Units: m², cm³

Read our dedicated guide: what is superscript, copy tables on superscript numbers copy paste, or use the live make raised exponents and footnotes tool.

Opposite of subscript and opposite of superscript

  • Opposite of subscript: superscript (above the line).
  • Opposite of superscript: subscript (below the line).

If you typed H₂O with a raised 2 by mistake, switch to Subscript on the tool and regenerate. If you need x² and typed a lowered 2, switch to Superscript.

When you need both in one line

Some formulas mix both: for example ions with charges (Na⁺ uses superscript) next to compounds with subscript counts (H₂O). Generate each piece in the correct mode on the tool, or use Formula mode for chemistry-style numbers, then paste the pieces together in Word or Docs.

Rule of thumb: atom counts and indices → subscript; powers, charges, and footnote markers → superscript.
