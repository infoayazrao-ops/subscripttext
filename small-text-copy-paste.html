Why shrinking the font fails

Instagram and Discord ignore a smaller point size from Word. They need a different Unicode letter. q has no tiny form — it stays a normal q so you do not get a box.

These letters are modifier / superscript Latin, not a new font file. That is why they survive a paste into a bio, a nickname, or a caption. It is also why they are not the same as H₂O (lowered digits) or a Word “shrink font” trick.

If you highlight “hello” in Word, drop the point size to 8, and copy, the chat still shows a normal hello. The app never received a new character — only a style that died at the paste. The chips above send real code points. After you paste, you should see ʰᵉˡˡᵒ, not a smaller Times New Roman.

Some fonts draw those marks a bit higher or thinner. That is the font, not the app deleting your bio. If you see a box, the device is missing that one letter. Switch the field back to a common system font, or leave the missing letter normal.

How to paste on a phone or computer

  1. Tap a word above. The chip should read Copied.
  2. Open the app. Long-press the bio, chat, or caption box and tap Paste.
  3. On a computer, click the chip, then Ctrl + V (Windows) or Cmd + V (Mac).

Instagram’s bio field still counts each tiny letter as one character toward the 150 limit. Discord nicknames are shorter: 32 characters, so a long aesthetic line will not fit. Captions and chat boxes are longer; a full sentence usually will.

If Paste is greyed out, focus the text field first, then copy the chip again. In-app browsers sometimes block the clipboard — use the real Instagram or Discord app, or a normal desktop browser.

TikTok captions and display names take the same paste. A comment field usually keeps the glyphs; some sticker fonts fall back to regular Latin, so check the preview. You do not need a “tiny font” download. If a letter looks off, it is almost always q (no tiny form) or a theme font that cannot draw that mark.
