Raised text, not a smaller font

What is superscript? It is text above the baseline — x², E=mc², or a footnote marker ¹. Shrinking the font is not the same thing. Unicode raised characters stay when you copy between Word, Google Docs, and chat.

Quick answer: Use it for powers, footnotes, and units. Type x2 in the live converter, copy, and paste.

Published 7 May 2026 · Updated 24 August 2026

When do you need it?

  • Math powers: x², y³, aⁿ
  • Science: E=mc², 10⁶
  • Units: m², cm³
  • Footnotes: markers ¹ ² ³

Teachers and students use the raised 2 most often: area, “squared,” and the 2 in E=mc². A footnote ¹ is the same family of characters, not a lowered ₁ from chemistry.

Slides and posters need the same mark if classmates will copy the text out. A screenshot of a Word equation will not give them a pasteable ². Give them the character, or they will retype a normal 2.

Why Word’s raised 2 dies in chat

Ctrl + Shift + = in Word makes a normal 2 look high. Paste that sentence into WhatsApp or a comment box and the 2 usually drops back. The app never received a raised character — only a style that stayed inside the document.

² is a different code point (U+00B2). Once it is on the clipboard, most phones and browsers keep it up. If an old font shows a box, the device is missing that glyph. Switch to a common system font; do not shrink the point size again.

Papers can keep Word styling if the file never leaves Word. The moment you copy into email, a LMS comment box, or a group chat, you need the Unicode mark. Units such as 25 m² and notes such as “see ¹” are the lines people actually send. Do not type ^2 and hope the app will raise it.

On a phone, copy ² or x², long-press the message box, Paste. On a computer, click, then Ctrl + V or Cmd + V. If you still see a full-size 2, you copied a normal digit.
